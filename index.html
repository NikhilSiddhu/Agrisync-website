What is AgriSync

A smartwatch for the field, not a diagnosis after the fact.

Most tools name a disease only once it's visible on a leaf — when the spray window is already half closed. AgriSync learns what one healthy cabbage field looks like and flags the drift, aiming to warn 12–72 hours before the eye can catch it.

A 12–72 hour risk curveRising and falling infection pressure for Alternaria leaf spot and downy mildew, from published science.
Learns healthy, flags the driftIt models a healthy field, so it needs no library of "sick plant" photos to raise the alarm.
One field, one seasonOne farm in Hapur, UP; one crop; two diseases — finished and proven, not half-built across many.

A smallholder can lose most of a crop in a single week. The data is the product — the predictor is the proof that it works.

How it works

One box in the field, one Pi at home.

A solar node reads the canopy every five minutes and sends it home over a cellular modem — there is no Wi-Fi in the field. A Raspberry Pi 5 stores every reading, and from October it will fuse them with free satellite and weather data. About ₹8,000 of parts. No mains power, no cloud bill.

01

A box in the field

Air temperature, humidity, pressure and rain — read at canopy height every five minutes, right where infection actually happens. Dew point is derived from the first two.

02

Free satellites

Sentinel-2 reads crop stress from orbit; Sentinel-1 radar sees through monsoon cloud. Both free, both field-scale. Ingestion is built in October.

03

Free weather

Decades of hourly history to learn from (ERA5-Land, NASA POWER), plus the 12–72 hour forecast (Open-Meteo) that makes prediction possible. Also October.

04

A Pi 5 at home

It stores every reading and runs the engine, and will pull in the outside data once that lands. The database sits on the Pi itself — no cloud bill, no third-party outage, nothing to keep paying for after the season.

05

A 12–72h warning

The output is a rising risk curve for the next one to three days — an honest window, not a false-precise single hour.

06

A growing dataset

The headline deliverable: a season of leaf-level readings paired with dated records of what the plants actually did.

Leaf wetness is inferred, not measured. The infrared sensor meant to read leaf-surface temperature never worked reliably and was removed, so wetness is estimated from air temperature and dew point instead. Both infection models depend on it, which makes this the biggest compromise in the build.

Intelligence

Two stages, one honest answer.

Known plant-science rules give a dependable floor from day one. A learned "healthy baseline" catches what the rules miss — and every answer comes as a calibrated range, not a fake-precise number.

Stage 1 · Physics

Infection-pressure rules

Published temperature and leaf-wetness models score disease pressure live. Works on day one, no training data — a 60–70% floor. Modest, but honest and immediate.

Stage 2 · Anomaly

Learn healthy, detect the drift

It models a healthy field across sensor and satellite signals, then flags the departure — so it needs no library of disease-labelled examples.

The check · Attribution

Disease, or just stress?

A drift alone could be drought or hunger. It's confirmed only when the weather sits in the infection window and the satellite bands agree.

The honesty · Uncertainty

A calibrated risk band

Risk is a range with a stated error rate across the 12–72 hour window. Act on "risk is climbing over two days" — nobody should act on a made-up 87%.

Status · August 2026

What's built, and what isn't.

The station went up in Hapur this month and has been uploading since. The box, the link and the pipeline are done. The outside data and the part that learns arrive in October.

Working today

The station, end to end

Both boards flashed and running on solar. Cellular upload with every request signed, so no shared secret crosses the wire and a dead link is buffered rather than lost. A camera photographing the same leaves on a fixed interval. Database, ingestion, the infection model for both diseases, the risk API, and a public dashboard with a signed-in view of the full record.

Still to come

Outside data, and the learned part

From October: the weather feeds (ERA5-Land, NASA POWER, Open-Meteo) and Sentinel-1 / Sentinel-2 ingestion. Then direct leaf-wetness measurement, which the models really want, and the healthy-baseline anomaly detector — which cannot exist before there is a season of healthy baseline to train on. Calibrated uncertainty bands, then alerts.

The infection pressure on the dashboard is uncalibrated — it scores conditions against published thresholds and has never been checked against a real outbreak. Any accuracy figure before the season would still be a guess.

Why it matters

Built from one village field, for the farms nobody instruments.

Hyper-local

Every rule, threshold and healthy-baseline is tuned to one real cabbage field in Hapur, UP — not a generic, all-crop model.

Built for the overlooked

Per-plot rigs cost a fortune and target vineyards; satellite platforms sell generic data to corporates. No deployed product combines pre-symptom prediction, one crop and cheap shared hardware for a smallholder.

Cheap by design

The satellites, the weather and the science are free or open. The box is about ₹8,000 of parts, solar-powered and off-grid — no mains and no cloud bill. The only running cost is a prepaid SIM.

Built solo from one family's cabbage field in Hapur, on minimum resources — the first dataset of its kind, made by hand.
