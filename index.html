What goes in

Six sources. One answer.

Each covers what the others cannot see.

  • Phytoplex, on a pole in the crop, measures temperature and humidity among the leaves, signs each reading and sends it home over 4G. When it misses an hour, the gap is filled from weather models shifted by this field's own offset at that time of day.

    BME280every 5 minsince 11 Aug 2026

  • The ECMWF ensemble runs the same forecast 51 ways. Each run is corrected by what Phytoplex has learned about this field's nights, cooler and damper than the grid, and nudged by how far off the forecast is right now.

    ECMWF ensemblenext 72 hdew point error 2.9 → 1.8 °C

  • Whether a leaf is wet depends on how much heat it loses to the sky and how much the wind gives back. The leaf's energy balance is run hour by hour with cloud, wind and sunshine, so a clear still night counts as wet and a cloudy breezy one does not.

    Penman-Monteithdew, rain, drying

  • Published trials say how many wet hours each disease needs at each temperature. Alternaria infects after about 4 wet hours at 18 °C. Downy mildew needs 4 wet hours in cool weather. White mould needs 23 humid hours in a row.

    AlternariaDowny mildewBlack rotWhite mould

  • Sentinel-2 measures how green the field is on clear days; Sentinel-1 radar sees it through cloud. A closing canopy keeps lower leaves wet longer, and a canopy falling in disease weather raises the odds.

    Sentinel-2Sentinel-1 radar10 m pixels

  • Every walk through the crop is logged, healthy ones included. Each one shifts the odds toward what this field really does. Spots seen on the farm raise the odds straight away, because the farm is now its own source of spores.

Composition

What it's made of.

Every part, and why it is there. Tap + to open one.

  • The home unit. Stores every reading, fetches the outside data each hour and runs the odds engine. No cloud bill.

  • The box's controller. Reads the sensors every five minutes and signs each upload, so nothing can be faked on the way.

  • Photographs the same leaves on a schedule, so the record has pictures as well as numbers.

  • Air temperature, humidity and pressure among the leaves. The dew point is worked out from the first two.

  • Turns analog signals into precise numbers. The leaf wetness sensor plugs in here.

  • Tells wet from dry when rain falls on it. It does not measure millimetres. This one has read wet since 27 September and is replaced after the season.

  • There is no Wi-Fi in the field. Every reading travels home over the mobile network.

  • Keeps the box running day and night with no mains power.

  • Will measure wet leaves directly instead of computing them. Goes in after the season.

  • European satellite that sees colour. Measures how green the field is on clear days.

  • Radar sees through cloud, so the canopy is followed even when Sentinel-2 cannot see it.

  • 51 versions of the same forecast. Their spread becomes the range around the odds.

  • Free access to the forecasts, and to what earlier forecasts said, which is how the field correction learns.

  • Hour-by-hour weather history. Fills the hours the station missed and supplies cloud, wind and sunshine.

  • Daily sunlight records for the field, as a cross-check.

  • Runs the season and the next 72 hours 1,020 times with the uncertain numbers varied, and averages them into one chance.

  • Works out dew and drying from radiation, wind and humidity, hour by hour.

  • The wet hours and temperatures five cabbage diseases need, from published trials, in Magarey's generic infection model.

  • Learns how this field differs from the forecast and corrects the next one, from the last 30 days only, because the offset moves with the season.

  • Moves the odds toward what the field log shows. Called checked after 20 walks with at least 3 outbreaks.

  • Flags any hour far from the same hour over the last 15 days.

  • Points at disease only when the canopy falls while the weather favoured infection.

  • Names a part that is failing while the data still flows: a rain plate stuck wet, a sensor gone from the bus, a battery running low.

  • From firmware 1.6.0 the Pi sets how often the box reads, faster on wet nights, and sends it new firmware between readings, checked and reversible. Goes on the box after the season.

  • The database on the Pi. Every reading since 11 August, kept at home.

  • Puts the Pi online without opening any port on the home network.

Built by one person, from one family's cabbage field in Hapur.

Disease forecasting from field sensors exists, mostly as paid services for high-value crops. AgriSync tests whether Phytoplex, a ₹12,000 unit in the crop, and free public data can give a smallholder the same early warning.

Built in the open, one season at a time. Write if you work on plant pathology, remote sensing or small computers, or want this in another village.
